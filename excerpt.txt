Young Dandridge sat with his elbows rested on the kitchen table, his chin cradled in his short pudgy-fingered hands. His head and round face were smooth and completely shorn of hair. He was a little flushed — likely due to the warmth of the kitchen, where a comfortable yet insistent heat emanated from the wood-fuelled Rayburn that spanned the width of the inglenook fireplace. He looked to his left, through the small clear panes of the black wrought iron window, its three casements set between yellow stone mullions paled by vestiges of worn lime whitewash, and out across the walled garden, lit bright by a sun in a clear and cloudless sky.

***

He turned slowly to the right, his eyes taking in the ceramic green Rayburn and smooth uneven plastered walls, until his gaze came to rest upon Uncle Jimmy in his corner chair. Young Dandridge looked across the kitchen table taking in the sight of the man reclining in the armchair. It had four legs of smoky pale wood, each atop a brass paw — not turned or carved but as though the bark had simply been peeled from the bough and sanded smooth, each one rising lustrous with polished layers of lime wax and the patina of an eternal rubbing by small furry pets. The upholstery of the chair was grimy, faded and threadbare in some places. Smooth with a build-up of dirt and grease, from the soft caresses of its occupants in others. The fabric looked as though it may have, some decades before, been reddish or dark salmon pink velvet, embroidered with plants and birds of greens, blues and yellows.

***

[[image: assets/kitchen_1.jpg | Young Dandridge and Uncle Jimmy at the kitchen table, beneath the wrought iron window and beside the green Rayburn]]

***

Uncle Jimmy was settled back in the chair fingers interlaced across his belly, legs extended straight, crossed at the ankles. The left heel of his wheat colour ankle boot rested on the smooth grey flagstone floor. The right boot rested upon the left, with laced insteps at 90 degrees to one another. The bootleg hems of his faded blue jeans covered the top of the boots, encasing sturdy legs. Above them, a chunky brass buckle, fastening a brown leather belt, was visible between the double-u of the twin v of his bright royal blue velvet waist coat. Which stretched across a broad chest, bottom silver button undone. His burgundy shirt was open at the collar, the top three buttons unfastened, as were the cuffs, rolled up to just below the elbow of large darkly tanned forearms, lightly covered with fine golden hair.

***

Above the shirt, his head was tilted slightly back against the high upholstered chair. Only the lower part of his face — from bearded chin to the tip of his nose — was visible under the brim of his leather hat. The hat was in the style known since the mid-nineteen nineties as a deadman, a name derived from its appearance in a Jim Jarmusch film of the same title. Though a century before it had been called a John Bull, or dressage, topper. Uncle Jimmy's was oxblood from its slightly flared crown down five inches of upper, to a narrow curled brim of the darkest green — so dark it would have appeared black were it not separated from the top by a matt black band an inch high. His goatee was a dark copper with glints of gold, made patchy by a portion greying to white at the chin. It matched the tips of his sideburns, visible below the hat brim. Both were tidily trimmed to about a half inch, with a lighter stubble running across the cheeks and down to the longer hair of the same colour, sprouting tuftily but sparsely from his open shirt above the waistcoat. His tanned face was framed by long hair bleached to a lighter antique gold by the sun, hanging from inside the hat brim and flowing down over his shoulders.

***

Young Dandridge looked down at his similarly coloured boots and straight leg blue jeans, cut off just below the knees. He wore a blue checked short sleeved shirt buttoned to the collar, underneath an unbuttoned sleeveless, or "sawn off" denim jacket. Young Dandridge wore a thick curb silver necklace with a matching bracelet on his right wrist. Upon his left wrist glittered an enormous square gold wristwatch with several variously sized dials. His exposed hairless arms were not as tanned as Uncle Jimmy's, but far from anaemic.

***

[[image: assets/kitchen_2.jpg | Young Dandridge, elbows on the table, considering the room]]

***

Young Dandridge was not entirely sure of his own exact age. He was certain he was not a child and therefore, by process of elimination, a man — a young one, as his name suggested, so likely not an awful long way past thirty. He was less sure about the man in the armchair. Young Dandridge knew he was younger than Uncle Jimmy, which implied that Uncle Jimmy was old — certainly older — though not elderly. Young Dandridge felt Uncle Jimmy far too vital for that description to fit. To an uninformed observer, the figure in the armchair might have been judged anywhere between his late forties and possibly even his early seventies.

***

Young Dandridge didn't really remember anything other than the kitchen, which the two of them inhabited. He knew the kitchen was part of what was called the cottage, which contained a number of bedrooms, a dining room, a lounge, a study and a large library. It was very likely a Manor House, though not quite grand enough to qualify as a stately home. While he was fully aware of the rest of the cottage, he could only really remember having been in the kitchen — what was inside it and the view from its window.

***

In his armchair in the corner, Uncle Jimmy stirred. He placed his hands on the armrests and pushed himself upright. His head came forward from the back of the chair, and he pushed his hat back, higher on his head, bringing more of his tanned, deeply lined face into view. Black wrap-around sunglasses shielded his eyes.

***

'Elbows off table,' snapped Uncle Jimmy, in his deep low voice.

Young Dandridge sat up straighter.

'Today,' said Uncle Jimmy, 'we are going to talk about everything. We're going to examine how it all works.'

'I'm all ears, Uncle Jimmy.' Young Dandridge looked across the table expectantly.
